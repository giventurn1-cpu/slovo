360p is the number that matters

A watermark is a cosmetic problem; 360p is a structural one. Music visualizers are made of gradients, particles, glow and grain — exactly the content that bands and smears at low resolution and low bitrate. A 360p export of a good template looks cheap regardless of the template, which is why almost nobody publishes one. On Renderforest the fix is a paid tier. On SLOVO there is no cap to lift: 720p, 1080p and 4K are all free, because rendering happens on your machine and costs Renderforest nothing to give away — it is simply not their model.

Where Renderforest wins

Breadth. If the same subscription also has to produce a logo, a mockup, an intro animation and a landing page, Renderforest does all of that and SLOVO does none of it. Its template library is large and its stock assets are included. And, like every server-rendered tool, it does not care how slow your computer is — the export takes the same time on any machine.

Where SLOVO wins

On the one job they share. Watermark-free export at any resolution, any length, without an account. Visuals driven by separate bass, transient and spectrum analysis rather than a waveform, so the motion follows the music rather than decorating it. Your own photo or video as the background, with depth separation so the scene moves behind the subject. Lyrics timed to the vocal after you paste them. Three aspect ratios, a Spotify Canvas loop and a thumbnail from one project. And the track never leaves your device, because there is nowhere for it to go.

Which to pick

Pick Renderforest if you need a whole brand kit from one subscription, or you want server rendering on a slow machine and do not mind paying to lift the caps.

Pick SLOVO if the job is a music video — official audio, a feed clip, a lyric video, a Canvas — and you want it watermark-free, at full resolution, without uploading the track.
