No account linking, on purpose

SLOVO does not connect to Suno and does not ask for a login there. You export the finished audio the way you already do, and hand that file to a tool that runs locally. Nothing is linked, nothing is authorised, and no third service gets a copy of your track. For music you may not have published yet, that matters more than one saved click.

You already have the lyrics — use them

This is where AI-generated tracks have an unfair advantage over recorded ones. With a normal recording you have to transcribe the vocal by ear before you can make a lyric video. With a prompted song the exact text is already in your hands. Paste it in, play the track and tap the space bar as each line begins — one listen and the whole song is timed. Show a line at a time, or let the Karaoke style fill each line from left to right while it is on screen.

Worth knowing: SLOVO deliberately does not run speech recognition or guess the timing. Over a mix both misread the vocal, and they normally mean shipping audio to someone else's server. You supply the words — which you already have — and the timing, which takes one listen. Details on the lyric video maker page.

Use the cover art you generated

Save the artwork alongside the audio and put it in the scene. It can sit in the centre and pulse with the track, the way official audio uploads look, or become the full background with visuals layered over it. 3D Photo goes further: a neural network builds real depth from the image in your browser, separates the subject, fills in what was behind it and moves the camera around it — a flat AI-generated image stops looking flat.

In the centre, OFFICIAL can put the cover over a liquid background made from the same art: it ripples on its own and sinks into the depth with the bass. And if the song came with several images — alternative covers, frames you generated for the mood — drop up to 30 of them and they become a slideshow whose cuts land on the beats of the track.

Calm or on the beat

AI-generated songs range from ambient to hard trap, and the visual should follow the genre. One button in the top bar switches every hit on the beat off at once — the picture, the effect and the lyrics stop pulsing and only slow motion is left, which suits a ballad or a lo-fi track. Switch it back on for anything with drums worth showing.

One track, every place you publish

YouTube — 16:9 with cover art, artist name and title. A 1280×720 thumbnail can be built from any frame of the track.

TikTok, Reels and Shorts — 9:16, with platform safe zones respected so your title does not end up under the share button. Trim to the hook, or cut one track into several clips with different visuals.

Spotify — a 3–8 second silent vertical loop in the exact Canvas format.

Make the video not look generated

AI tracks get published in enormous volume, and most of them ship with the same static square cover. Two things separate a post that gets watched from one that gets scrolled past. First, movement that matches the music: SLOVO reads the low end, the transients and the spectrum separately, so the scene jolts on the kick and flares on the drop instead of wobbling generically. Second, text on screen, because a large share of feed views happen with the sound off.

A note on disclosure
