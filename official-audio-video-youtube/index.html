How to make an official audio video for YouTube

“Official audio” is the upload that stands in for a music video when there is no music video: the recording, the cover art, the artist name and the title, on the artist's own channel. This guide covers what it is, how it differs from the Art Track your distributor may already have generated, what YouTube recommends for the file, and how to build one that looks official in a few minutes.

Official audio versus Art Track

YouTube describes an Art Track as “an automatically generated YouTube version of a track on a music album” that “consists of the sound recording and the album art”. It is created for label and distributor partners through Content Manager, not uploaded by the artist, and it is what appears on the auto-generated “Topic” channels. It is useful — it exists for every track on every album — but it is a static square over a recording, and it does not live on your channel.

An official audio upload is yours. It sits on your channel with your subscribers, it can be scheduled with the release, it carries your branding, and — the part that matters for retention — it can move with the music. Most artists publish both: the Art Track arrives on its own through the distributor, and the official audio is the version they actually share.

What makes it look official

  • Cover art in the centre, square, sharp, with room around it. This is the layout every listener recognises from streaming.
  • Artist and title on screen — the video should identify itself even when embedded without a caption.
  • Motion that follows the track, not a timer. A ring that pulses with the spectrum, particles on the kick, a subtle breathing of the artwork. A static image reads as an Art Track; a moving one reads as a release.
  • A consistent look across the catalogue: same layout, same typeface, different art. A channel where every audio matches looks like a label.
  • A 1280×720 thumbnail that is the cover, not a random frame.

Resolution and length

1080p is the sensible default and what most audiences watch at. 4K earns its render time when the visual has fine texture — particles, film grain, smooth gradients — because those band and smear at lower bitrates; a plain cover on a dark background gains nothing from it. The video should be the full track: an official audio is the place people go to hear the whole thing, and cutting it short reads as a teaser.

Title, description, tags

The convention is Artist – Title (Official Audio). It matches how people search and tells them this is the canonical upload rather than a rip. In the description, put the streaming links first, then credits, then lyrics — the lyrics help the video surface for people searching a line they remember. If the music was made with AI, check YouTube's current disclosure rules; they change.
