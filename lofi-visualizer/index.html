Why lo-fi videos are built differently

Lo-fi is background music by design, and its videos are watched for a long time at low attention — a study session, a late shift, a rainy afternoon. A visual that demands attention breaks the contract. The scenes here move slowly, repeat gracefully and never flash; the beat reaction is there, but tuned so a kick registers as a breath rather than a jolt. Over a still photograph of a room, a desk or a street, the effect is the classic lo-fi channel look without the animation budget.

Tape and film

CASSETTE and POCKET give you the object the genre is named after; RADIO adds the amber dial. FILM and PERFORATION bring projector flicker and 35 mm edges, VHS brings tracking noise. Each is a way of saying analogue without a filter pack, and each reads instantly at feed size.

Making a long one

SLOVO exports the full length of whatever you drop in, and desktop Chrome or Edge renders faster than real time. Tracks up to about twelve minutes are the comfortable range; for an hour-long mix, export it in parts and join them, or loop a shorter export. Keep the reaction low, put your own photo behind the widgets, and let the tape do the work.
